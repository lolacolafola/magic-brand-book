Standing rules

Three rules that override everything else in this document.

  • Nothing public ships in French without approval from the French voice gate. French that has not been through the gate is treated as untranslated.
  • Product names never translate. In any language, in any market. See Lexicon.
  • Nothing here names a competitor, and no outside product is cited as a reference or an inspiration. This document travels. Comparisons stay in conversation.
  • Magic is never positioned as an AI company or an AI platform. Not to fans, not to artists, not to their teams. Say what a thing does. The technology behind it belongs in investor and partner conversations and nowhere else.

Section

Positioning

What Magic is, what it promises, and the lines that carry it.

Vision

What we love should bring us together, not keep us scrolling.

The emotional core, and the standard every product decision is measured against.

Mission

Mission

Help people live their passions fully, together, and on their own terms.

Used in full. It fits a slide, a bio and a sentence spoken out loud.

Tagline

Tagline

LIVE IT.
FOR REAL.

Two halves. LIVE IT is the instruction. FOR REAL is the promise.

LIVE IT

Two words, and both are working.

  • LIVE is the mission’s verb. The mission is to help people live their passions fully. The tagline is the mission in two words, using the same word on purpose.
  • It is an instruction. The line tells a fan to do something, and the fan is the subject of the sentence.
  • IT names nothing. The word is deliberately empty. It means whatever the person is into, which is the same breadth that lets a Fanverse be built around a football club or a beauty creator.
  • It answers the vision. What we love should bring us together, not keep us scrolling. Living it is the opposite of scrolling past it.

LIVE IT never changes

The tagline is never rewritten. Feel it. For real. is a different line and belongs to a different brand.

The frame below is a separate device. It borrows FOR REAL and leaves LIVE IT alone, because FOR REAL is the half that attaches to any verb.

FOR REAL

For real, as in finally

The way people already say it. Being a fan has gone unseen and unrewarded for years. FOR REAL says it counts now.

For real, as in off the screen

The move out of the app and into the world. Actual gigs, actual meetups, actual things in a fan's hand.

Both meanings at once, or the line stops working. A phrase fans already say happens to describe exactly what Magic does, which is take fandom off the screen and into the world. On its own the first meaning is a slogan. On its own the second is a feature description.

You [do the thing], for real.

LineWhere it belongs
You live your passions, for real.Brand line. The closest restatement of the mission.
You connect with your favourite artists, for real.Magic Fanverses
You build connections, for real.Friends, and the fan-to-fan story
You get rewarded, for real.Magic Rewards
You discover what you actually care about, for real.Magic Companion

Each one carries both senses. "You get rewarded, for real" promises that the reward is genuine and that it exists in the world. That is the test for any new line in the frame.

Limits on the frame

  • Once per surface. One line in the frame per page, screen, post or email. Repeated, it stops being a promise and becomes a tic.
  • The verb has to be something a fan does. "You live", "you connect", "you get". Never something Magic does.
  • Both senses or neither. If the line only works as emphasis, cut it. That is a slogan wearing the tagline's clothes.
  • The comma stays. It is the pause the whole line turns on.
  • Never end it with an exclamation mark. The phrase carries its own weight.
  • The tagline rarely travels alone. Attached to a fandom object it could only be Magic. Default to the frame; reserve the bare tagline for the logo lockup and moments that already carry the context.

The frame in French

The tagline itself stays English. The construction is the harder question, because "for real" is doing something specific that most translations lose.

One candidate for the French voice gate to judge: en vrai may carry both senses. The gate decides whether it does. Tu vis tes passions, en vrai. Whether that lands as natural or as a translation is a call only a native speaker can make, and it needs making before any French campaign uses the frame.

What is settled: pour de vrai is not it. It reads as something said to a child.

Why two full stops

The line is a promise, and two complete beats land as a promise. One beat and a trailing fragment lands as an aside.

The practical reason matters more. A line ending without a stop reads as an error to everyone who did not write it, so designers, agencies and new joiners will add it, and correcting that forever costs more than the distinction is worth. A rule that has to be defended constantly is the wrong rule.

Supersedes the brand platform, which sets the tagline three different ways across four pages: without the final stop, without the space after it, and inside guillemets. None of those is the form.

What Magic is

One app for everything your passions deserve: personalised recommendations, real fan communities, real rewards.

Magic is not an AI platform

Magic is never described to a fan or an artist as an AI company, an AI platform or an AI product. How it works is never explained to them either.

The technology belongs in investor and partner conversations. Nowhere a fan or a talent team can read it.

A fan does not want an AI companion. They want something that knows what they are into. An artist’s manager does not want an AI platform. They want to know which thousand of their listeners drive the business.

Describe the outcome. Never the machinery.

What it costs when we break it

  • It hands the fan a reason to distrust the thing. "The algorithm picked this" is the opposite of "someone who gets me picked this." The moment a recommendation is machinery, it stops being a recommendation.
  • It puts Magic in the category fans already resent. A feed optimised against them is the thing they are leaving. Saying Magic runs on the same technology invites the same suspicion.
  • It is the one claim every competitor makes. Leading on it identifies nobody.
  • It argues against the tagline. FOR REAL promises something genuine. Machine-generated is what a fan hears as the opposite.

The Magic journey

The organising idea underneath the products. Magic creates universes for fans, and one can be built around anything a person loves.

The idea

Magic creates universes for fans.

Fan and universe. Fanverse.

Fan+universe=Fanverse
FanThe person, and the thing they love. Someone with a fandom.
UniverseA whole world, with its own people, language and things to do. Somewhere you go, and can come back to.

A universe built for the fans of one thing, made by Magic and filled by them.

What a Fanverse is

A Fanverse is a universe built around one passion. Magic creates it. The fans of that thing live in it.

It can be built around anything anyone is passionate about. An artist. A football club. A game. A show. A beauty creator. A festival. The passion decides the world, and nothing in the format limits what that passion can be.

That breadth is the point of the word. Music is the first category through the door, and a Fanverse works the same way for a club, a game or a creator. Any copy that treats Fanverse as a music term has made the idea smaller than it is.

Two things to hold

Magic is the creative act. Magic builds these universes. That is the product, and copy should say it plainly.

The universe is for fans. It exists so the fans of a thing have somewhere that belongs to that thing. Everything inside it is built for them.

How fans behave

The fan audience, split by what a fan does.

Three groups

“Fans, GenZ” is not a target a writer can aim at. Every fanbase is a pyramid: most watch, some take part, a few build. Three groups, split by what a fan does, because behaviour is what copy can change.

GroupRoughlyWhat they wantWhat the copy has to do
Watching
the quiet majority		~55%Something to do with the fandom they already have, at no cost and no commitmentOffer one small action. No jargon, nothing that reads as joining a club. They already love the artist who sent them.
Taking part
the daily returners		~30%Proof that showing up counted, and first access to the things they collectShow the outcome and name what changed. Be exact about what is in it, how many exist, and when it drops.
Building
the core		~15%To be seen after years, recognised as someone who brings people inAddress them as a host. Give them something to hand to other people. Never imply status can be bought.

Almost everyone arrives already committed

Most people install because an artist they love told them to. They walk in through a door that artist opened, and they are not drifting when they get here.

So first-open copy assumes the love and skips the persuading. Watching describes how active someone is inside Magic. It says nothing about how much they care, and at arrival they usually care a great deal.

Two different things, easy to blur: how deep a person is in a fandom, and how active they are in the app. These three groups describe the second one only.

These are not a product feature

Watching, Taking part and Building describe how a fan already behaves. They exist for whoever is writing. No fan is ever shown them, told which one they are in, or moved between them.

Avoid the word tier for them. Magic’s levels and passes are still being worked on, and when they land, tier will mean something a fan climbs or buys.

How to read the percentages

They come from published participation research on how any online community splits. Magic has no fan data of its own yet. Treat them as the shape of a fanbase, and no Magic-specific fan figure appears in public copy until Magic Pulse produces one.

Write down the pyramid

Watching is the biggest group and the one every fan platform ignores, because platforms are built by people who already behave like the core. Copy written to impress the core will not reach them. Copy written for Watching still works on everyone above it.

Artist-facing

Magic is sold to the talent side, and the person who signs is usually not the artist.

The thing to hold on to

The artist is the reason. The manager is the buyer.

Artist-facing copy that addresses the star flatters the wrong reader. At every stage above the earliest, someone whose job is the business reads it first and decides whether it goes further.

Three buyers

BuyerWho actually signsThe problem in their wordsWhat the copy leads with
Rising independent
emerging artist		The artist, or their one manager“My real fans are out there and I cannot find them.”What they get in the first week, with no budget and no team. Speed and cost, before capability.
Star and team
signed, at scale		Manager or label marketing lead“Millions of listeners, and no idea which thousand drive the business.”Resolution. Telling the day ones apart from the drive-bys, and controlling what each group gets.
Festival or rights-holder
event and brand		Marketing or partnerships lead“We sell out every year and lose contact the next morning.”The relationship that survives the event, and an owned audience a sponsor can be sold.

Same product, three different first sentences. Leading the festival with fan identification, or the independent with audience tiering, wastes the only paragraph that gets read.

How artist-facing copy sounds

The personality does not change. The register does. Every line in Voice still applies. Being real, specific and plain matter more to this reader.

Does

Names the mechanic. Carries the number and where it came from. Assumes commercial literacy and a short attention span. Talks about the artist’s business.

Does not

Borrow fan slang. Use the mascot. Promise a result Magic has not measured. Explain the music industry to someone who works in it.

Do not lead with owning the fanbase

Every platform in this category makes that promise, which makes it the price of entry. It can appear as context. It cannot be the headline.

The claim that is actually ours is what a fan does daily. The habit, the return visit, the fan who is still there between releases. That is the part competitors cannot copy from a feature list, and it is the part Magic can evidence.

State mechanics, not results

A mechanic can be stated without evidence. A result cannot. Until a claim has a source that survives a manager asking where the number came from, describe what Magic does and stop there.

Section

Business

Investors, technology partners, trade press and the people Magic hires.

This is the only place the technology is the story

Everywhere else, Magic describes what a thing does. Here, how it works is the point, because defensibility is what this audience is buying. See Positioning.

Two things follow, and the second matters more.

  • Business material is written to be read by this audience alone. Market size, retention curves, the engine, the moat. None of it is written to be readable by a fan, because a fan is never going to read it.
  • It never leaks sideways. The real failure happens outside the deck. Language written for an investor gets reused on a stage, on a podcast or in a fan community, because it is the language whoever is speaking has most recently rehearsed.

The test before speaking

Who is in the room, and could a fan hear this? A stage, a panel, a podcast, a Discord and an open community call are all fan-facing, whatever the invitation said. If a fan could hear it, the fan-facing rules apply, and the technology stays out of it.

Voice

One personality, two registers. The personality holds across both audiences; the register changes.

The personality

Four traits, carried over from the 2025 brand platform. They stand until something replaces them, and they hold across both audiences.

We areBut neverWhat that means when writing
RealStagedEvery claim names something the product does today. Urgency is fine when it is true, and never invented.
YouthfulTrying too hardWe speak the language because we are in the culture.
EmpoweringPreachyHand over the tools. No lectures about what fandom should be.
PlayfulSillyWit, and it never undercuts the moment a fan is having.

Two more, added because both have already gone wrong in Magic copy. Specific: name the artist, the drop, the date. Plain: short sentences, everyday words.

The two registers

Fan register

Second person, contractions, cultural reference assumed. Reads like a friend who knows the scene. Used on social, in-product, and in anything a fan sees.

Industry register

Third person or first-person plural, no slang, every claim carrying its number and its source. Used on LinkedIn, in decks, and in partner conversations.

The industry register on a fan reads like a brochure. The fan register on a label reads like a pitch from someone junior.

Section

Copy rules

These are enforced. Work that breaks them is sent back regardless of who wrote it.

Binding

Two rules, both from real failures in Magic’s own materials.

  1. No negation-contrast construction. No "X is a Y, not a Z". No "It isn't A. It's B". State the thing itself. One exception below.
  2. Product names never translate. In any language, in any market.

The rules on percentages, taxonomies, cross-references and commitments were written for investor and board decks, where they belong. They live in the working context, not here.

Writing it

The mechanics. These settle the questions that come up with a caption open and twenty minutes to write it.

Fan-facingArtist-facing
PersonSecond person, singular. The copy addresses one fan.Third person, or first-person plural for Magic.
ContractionsYes. It is how people speak.No.
Sentence lengthShort, and varied. A four-word sentence next to a long one. Even length is the deepest AI tell.
CapitalisationSentence case everywhere. Product names always capitalised. The tagline is the only thing set in full caps.
EmojiDepends on the format. See below.Never.
Exclamation marksOne per piece at most, and never in a headline. Never on the tagline. The mascot is the exception and speaks in its own voice.
NumbersFigures for anything a fan acts on: dates, times, prices, counts. Words for one to nine in running prose.
SpecificityName the artist, the venue, the date. A line that would work for any artist has not been written yet.

Magic is never sold as AI. The rule and the substitutions are in Positioning, because it decides what Magic is before it decides how a sentence reads.

Where the energy comes from

The rules above are all prohibitions, and copy written only to them breaks nothing and does nothing. The personality says Playful and Youthful. These are the rules that get it there.

  • Specific is funnier than general. "A trivia game about her cats" lands. "An engaging quiz experience" does not. Almost every flat line in Magic copy is a line that got vaguer to sound safer.
  • Urgency is allowed when it is true. Fifty exist and they go Thursday, so say so. The ban is on inventing scarcity, never on stating it.
  • Lead with the thing. Open on the artist, the object, the moment. Framing goes second, if at all.
  • Write it to one person you know. If a line would be strange to say out loud to a friend who loves that artist, it is not the line.
  • Short punch, then the detail. A four-word opener followed by the specifics reads as energy. Even-length sentences read as a press release.
  • The mascot runs hotter than the brand. Its animation set is welcoming, delighted and energised, and its voice matches. Brand copy stays a step cooler, and the mascot carries the enthusiasm.

Emoji, by format

One blanket rule was wrong. A push notification and a drop are conversion moments and they run hot. A bio is read once, slowly.

  • Push, drops, live moments. One or two, carrying the energy. They land after the fact, never before it. "Unreleased audio 🎧" works. "🎧 Unreleased audio" reads as a brand doing an impression.
  • Bios, captions, longer copy. Sparingly, and never in the first line.
  • Artist-facing. Never.

Emoji are punctuation for a fan audience. What makes a push convert is the artist’s name and the specific thing that just happened, and the emoji carries tone on top of that.

Vary them. One emoji used on everything becomes a tic the same way a repeated tagline does. Pick the one that matches the thing: 🎧 for audio, ⏳ for a deadline, 👀 for something worth looking at.

Written examples

Four pieces, written to the rules above. Copy the shape.

Push notification

Billie just dropped 3 minutes of unreleased studio audio 🎧 In her Fanverse, right now.

Why this one fails

An artist you follow has posted new exclusive content! Don’t miss out on this amazing moment 🎉

It names nobody and nothing. "An artist you follow" and "new exclusive content" fit any artist and any post, so a fan cannot tell whether it is worth opening. The emoji is not the problem. The absence of a name is.

Social bio

Everything you are into, in one place. Turn up, get rewarded for it, get to the moments that matter. LIVE IT. FOR REAL.

Why this one fails

Magic is the AI-powered fan engagement platform revolutionising how creators connect with their communities.

It describes the company to an investor. A fan reading a bio wants to know what they get. It also breaks the fourth standing rule: nothing fan-facing sells Magic on its technology.

Drop announcement

Only 50 of these exist. Made with her team, nowhere else on earth. Thursday 18:00 ⏳ then they’re gone.

Why this one fails

Limited edition drop coming soon! Set your alarms, you won’t want to miss this one.

No object, no number, no date. "Limited edition" and "coming soon" are urgency with no fact underneath, which is the invented scarcity the Real trait rules out. Fifty of a named thing at a stated time is more urgent, and it is true.

Artist-facing opener

You have 2.4 million monthly listeners and no way to tell which thousand drive your business. Magic gives your team that list, and a place to reach it.

Why this one fails

Own your fanbase. Take back control of your community from the platforms.

Every platform in the category says this. It identifies nobody, and it carries no number in a register that runs on evidence.

Nothing that reads as machine-written

Magic’s audience spots AI copy faster than any audience alive. The tagline promises FOR REAL. A brand that makes that promise and then writes like a language model has lost it.

NeverWhat it looks likeInstead
Em dashesThe clearest single tell there is. Anywhere, in any output, internal or public.A full stop, a comma or a colon.
The three-item rise"Faster, smarter, more connected."Two items, or four. Three lands as a cadence a machine reached for.

Section

French

France is the launch market. French is the copy that ships; English is the working language of this book. That order matters.

Do not reuse the French brand platform as source copy

It leaves English and French versions of every product name side by side, renders the vision line four different ways, and reads as translated. French is written, never translated. Everything public goes through the French gate.

How French works here

  • French is written, never translated. Brief the idea, write it in French, then check it against the English. Copy translated from English will sound translated, and a French music audience hears it immediately.
  • One approved rendering per key line. Vision, mission and tagline get a single agreed French form that never varies.
  • Product names stay English. Without exception.
  • The tagline stays English. LIVE IT. FOR REAL in every market.
  • Nothing public ships without the French voice gate. The CM alternant holds approval on all public French copy.

Four versions are in circulation. Use the rendering approved by the French gate, unchanged, everywhere.

Sand (E) is the sole warm value against six cool ones. That makes it the most powerful colour in the system and the easiest to waste. It carries one idea: everything a fan has earned.

How much of each

Knowing what a colour is for does not say how much of it to use, and that is where most off-brand work goes wrong. One colour leads. The rest support it.

ShareRoleIn practice
~80%The groundThe brand field, or a flat White, Ink or Night. One decision, made first.
~15%The leadThe colour the surface reads as. Usually Magic Blue, or White type on a dark ground.
~4%SupportSecondary elements. Azure, Mist, a container.
~1%The accentCyan or Sand. One thing on the surface, and it should be the thing that matters most.

Never more than three at once

Three palette colours plus the ground. A fourth is what makes a Magic asset look like a template with the brand colours poured into it. Over photography or the brand field, White leads and everything else is an accent.

Typography

Gilroy throughout, in three weights.

Gilroy ExtraBold · 800Display and section headings
Gilroy Bold · 700Titles, names, emphasis
Gilroy SemiBold · 600Values, specs, data
Gilroy Medium · 500Body copy, labels, navigation

Navigation labels run at 9px Gilroy Medium, small enough that it is worth checking against the smallest phone in the test set before launch.

How big the heading goes

Weights say which face to use and nothing about size. This is the missing half: three settings, chosen by how loud the piece needs to be. Sizes always step in multiples of two, so nobody has to exercise design judgement to get it right.

SettingHeading vs bodyUsed for
RecessiveSame sizeDense, informational surfaces where the reader is already reading. In-product detail, long documents, terms.
Informative2× or 4×The default. Most marketing, most decks, most social copy that carries an explanation.
Expressive6× or moreLaunch moments, covers, the tagline on the brand field. One idea, no explanation underneath it.

Anything Expressive with a paragraph under it is Informative pretending. Pick the setting from how much the piece has to say, then hold it across the whole asset.

When Gilroy is not available

Gilroy is licensed, so it cannot be embedded everywhere or installed by everyone. Without a named substitute, every deck shared outside Magic and every asset generated without the font falls back to whatever the machine happens to have, and the brand quietly dissolves.

The substitute is Outfit

An open licence, freely available, geometric like Gilroy and close enough in weight structure that the same hierarchy survives. It is what this book is set in wherever Gilroy is missing.

  • Use it where Gilroy cannot be installed or embedded: third-party decks, web where the licence does not reach, generated assets.
  • Never mix the two in one asset. Either everything is Gilroy or everything is Outfit. Mixed, the difference reads as a mistake.
  • Gilroy stays the brand face. Outfit is what keeps a stranger’s file on brand.

The brand field

Magic's brand moment is a deep blue gradient with a cool glow at one corner and a warm one at the other, carrying visible grain. It is the ground for feeling: social, covers, launch moments, the splash.

ElementValue

Cool glow#2E9AD8, one corner, wide and soft

Two glows, never more. The warm one is what stops the brand reading as cold tech, and it is the only place the palette's warmth appears at scale.

The mark
The markThe character. It carries Magic on its own.
The logoThe wordmark, Magic, set in Gilroy.
TogetherThe character above the wordmark, centred, as on the splash. Used for launch moments and covers.

What the mark means

The character is white because it belongs to no single Fanverse. It takes the colour of whichever world it is standing in, and it goes with the fan between them. A companion: present, following, never the main event, because in Magic the artist is.

The mascot

The character is Magic Companion. It is who a fan talks to, and the face of the product's most differentiated feature. It is white, and it holds the Home position in the tab bar, so the Companion is the app's front door.

That makes it load-bearing. It is the only thing in Magic that speaks in the first person, so its voice is a specification. See In-product voice.

Never call it a ghost

The word describes a shape and names nothing. It also carries associations no fan product wants, and one of them already owns a white mascot on the same channels Magic is launching into.

Until a name is agreed, it is the Magic mascot. Say the mark when the subject is the logo, and the mascot everywhere else. A name is coming.

In-product voice

The copy in the app predates the brand platform. All of it is in scope for rewriting.

Today, and why it fails

"Invite your friends and help the community grow."

Nothing in it could only have been written by Magic. It also names a benefit to the community, when the fan is the one being asked to act.

Written to these rules

Know someone who would love this Fanverse? Bring them in.

Rules for product copy

  • Say what happens. A button names its outcome; the confirmation uses the same word.
  • Second person, always. The app talks to one person.
  • Name the specific thing. "Billie Eilish at Accor Arena". Never "an event near you".
  • Empty states do a job. They say what will appear here and what to do to fill it.
  • Errors say what went wrong and how to fix it. No apology, no blame.
  • The mascot speaks; the interface does not. Personality lives in Companion's voice. Buttons and labels stay plain.

How Companion speaks

The mascot is the only first-person voice in Magic and the surface a fan spends the most time with. It carries the brand's personality.

  • Full of personality. It has opinions, enthusiasms and favourites. A neutral assistant would be the wrong character entirely.
  • Relentlessly positive. On your side, pleased you turned up, glad about what you are into. It never makes a fan feel behind.
  • Adds sparkle, within reason. Delight in small doses. A flourish that lands once is charming; the same flourish every turn is noise, and the fan stops reading.
  • Specific about what you love. "Billie's at Accor Arena in March". Never "here are some recommendations". Enthusiasm without a fact behind it is empty.
  • Short. One or two sentences at a time. It works in the rhythm of a chat.
  • Asks first. Its promise is getting sharper every session, which only works if it checks.
  • Admits what it doesn't know. A confident wrong recommendation costs more trust than a question does.

To rewrite the app

What's needed is the localisation file: one export of every UI string with its key, in English and French. With that, the rewrite is a single pass against these rules and the French gate. Without it, the work is done screen by screen from the design file, which is slower and misses every state that isn't drawn.
